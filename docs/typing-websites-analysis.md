## 2. First, Understand How WPM Works (Otherwise Comparisons Are Misleading)

- **WPM standard:** 1 "word" = 5 characters (including spaces). So WPM is really *characters per minute ÷ 5*.
- **Raw WPM vs. WPM:** Raw = all keystrokes; WPM = only correct ones. The gap between them = how much accuracy is costing you.
- **Different sites use different text**, so **the same person gets different scores on different sites**:
  - **10FastFingers** uses the top ~200 most common words → **easier text → inflated scores**.
  - **Monkeytype** (English 200/1k), quotes, punctuation, and numbers → progressively harder.
  - **Typing.io** uses real code with symbols and *includes backspace* → lowest "WPM" but most realistic for coding.
- **Rule:** Never compare your score across sites. Pick **one benchmark** (e.g. Monkeytype, 60 s, English) and track only that.

**Rough WPM benchmarks** (varies by source, use as a guide only):

| WPM | Level |
|---|---|
| < 30 | Beginner / hunt-and-peck |
| 40 | Average adult |
| 50–60 | Solid, professional-level |
| 70–90 | Fast |
| 100+ | Very fast (needs real touch typing + deliberate practice) |
| 150+ | Competitive / elite |

## 4. Site-by-Site Deep Dive

### 4.1 Monkeytype — *the benchmark tool*
🔗 monkeytype.com

**What it is:** A minimalist, highly customizable typing test. Open-source, free, no account needed (but an account saves history).

**Key features**
- **Test modes:** Time (15/30/60/120 s + custom), Words (10/25/50/100…), Quotes (short/medium/long), Zen, Custom text.
- **Modifiers:** Punctuation, numbers, multiple languages, and difficulty levels (Normal / Expert / Master — stricter about mistakes).
- **Stats:** Live WPM, raw WPM, accuracy, **consistency**, per-second graph, character breakdown.
- **Account:** Personal bests, history, streaks, daily/weekly leaderboards, XP/levels.
- **Fun stuff:** Funbox modes (40+ variations), hundreds of themes, sounds, smooth caret.
- **Code mode:** Language-specific code word lists (great for symbols practice).

**Pros**
- Cleanest and most trustworthy WPM measurement → best for tracking progress.
- Extremely customizable; you can build any practice session you want.
- Consistency and raw/WPM gap tell you *why* you're slow.
- Custom text lets you paste your own weak words or code.

**Cons**
- **Doesn't teach** touch typing. If you still look at the keyboard, start elsewhere.
- Easy to get obsessed with personal bests instead of practicing weaknesses.
- Free-form: you must design your own plan (this doc helps).

**Best use for speed:**
- **Weekly benchmark:** English 200, 60 s, no punctuation → record WPM, raw, accuracy, consistency.
- **Burst training:** 10-word or 15-second tests, go as fast as you can (ignore accuracy briefly to break your speed ceiling, then rebuild).
- **Real-world practice:** punctuation + numbers + quotes.

### 4.2 Keybr — *the smartest tutor*
🔗 keybr.com

**What it is:** An adaptive touch-typing trainer. It tracks every keypress, computes stats **per key**, and automatically generates lessons around your **weakest keys**.

**How it works**
- Starts with a small set of the most frequent letters in your language.
- **Unlocks more keys** only as you hit your target speed/accuracy on the current ones.
- Generates **readable pseudo-words** using the phonetic rules of the language (so you type things like "the", "that" and natural-feeling combos rather than random letters).
- Charts per-key speed, shows a calendar of practice days, supports different layouts (QWERTY, Dvorak, Colemak, etc.), and lets you set your own target speed.

**Pros**
- Removes the "which key am I bad at?" guesswork; the algorithm does it.
- Great for building **muscle memory and accuracy**.
- Free, no intrusive ads, open-source.
- Short sessions (10–20 min) work well.

**Cons**
- Pseudo-words aren't real language → doesn't fully prepare you for real text or punctuation (pair with Monkeytype).
- Minimal gamification; some people find it dry.
- Stats look slower than Monkeytype because words are harder/less predictable → don't panic about the lower number.

**Best for:** Beginners-to-intermediate (20–70 WPM) who want steady, measurable improvement. **If you only pick one training site, pick this one.**

### 4.3 TypingClub — *the classroom-style course*
🔗 typingclub.com

**What it is:** A structured, lesson-by-lesson touch typing course (by EdClub), widely used in schools, with individual accounts too.

**Key features**
- Step-by-step lessons: home row → other rows → numbers → punctuation → advanced.
- **Minimum score to pass** each lesson (you must hit an accuracy/speed target to move on).
- Games, videos, typing tests, badges/stars, progress tracking.
- Many languages/layouts, accessibility options (larger fonts, high-contrast, one-handed lessons).
- **Free edition** (ad-supported) + **Premium** (adds things like replay of attempts, extra reports, story typing, more games/themes). ⚠️ Pricing has varied across sources — one listing shows roughly **$78/year**; older articles show ~$6.50–$8.50/month. Check the site.
- School edition with class management (not relevant unless you're a teacher).

**Pros**
- Best "hand-holding" for people starting from zero.
- Clear structure and progress feel; hard to get lost.
- Teaches posture and finger placement properly.

**Cons**
- Fixed curriculum — not adaptive to *your* weak keys.
- Gamification is aimed at kids/students; can feel slow for adults.
- Premium features (replays, deep reports) cost money.

**Best for:** Absolute beginners or anyone who's never properly learned touch typing. Finish the course, then move to Keybr + Monkeytype.

### 4.4 10FastFingers — *the quick 1-minute benchmark*
🔗 10fastfingers.com

**What it is:** A simple 60-second typing test using random words from the **top ~200 most common words** in your language. Also has daily/regional leaderboards and 24-hour competitions.

**How it behaves**
- You don't need to fix typos, but **mistyped words don't count** unless corrected.
- Public competitions where everyone types the same text.
- Publicly cited record is in the 230s WPM range (⚠️ dated figure).

**Pros**
- Zero friction; quick daily test.
- Global leaderboard motivation and competitions.

**Cons**
- Common-word list = **easier than real text** → your WPM will look higher than on Monkeytype quotes or TypeRacer.
- Not a teacher; minimal analytics.
- Interface feels dated compared with Monkeytype.

**Best for:** A fun daily 1-minute check-in. Don't use it as your only benchmark.

---

### 4.5 TypeRacer — *real text under pressure*
🔗 typeracer.com

**What it is:** The original multiplayer typing game. You race other people typing **passages from books, movies, and songs**.

**Pros**
- Real sentences with punctuation and capitalization → closer to real-world typing.
- Racing others creates natural pressure, which helps break speed plateaus.
- Long-standing community, tournaments, detailed WPM/accuracy history, and a "practice" mode.

**Cons**
- Short races (~30–60 s) → limited endurance training.
- Not a tutor; you'll reinforce bad habits if you don't already touch type.
- Text quality/repetition: you'll see the same quotes over time.

**Best for:** Intermediate typists (40+ WPM) who want a competitive/pressure element.

---

### 4.6 Nitro Type — *typing as a racing game*
🔗 nitrotype.com

**What it is:** A gamified racing site — you and 4 other typists at a similar speed race with cars. Very popular (over a billion races since it launched in 2011, per one source).

**Key features**
- ~40-second texts; errors must be fixed before you can continue.
- **Points-based leaderboards** (speed + accuracy + volume), teams of up to 50, hundreds of achievements and cars to unlock.
- "Nitro" boost mechanic (press Enter to skip a word — fun, not real practice).
- Freemium (paid membership for cosmetics/perks).

**Pros**
- Highest motivation/"one more race" factor of all these sites.
- Volume of practice adds up — lots of repetitions.
- Team/friend competition keeps you consistent.

**Cons**
- Leaderboards reward **volume**, not just skill → you can climb by grinding.
- Game elements can distract from technique; easy to type sloppily for speed.
- Skews toward younger audience/school use.

**Best for:** Students who need a gamified reason to practice daily. Use it for **fun volume**, not as your main training.

---

### 4.7 Typing.io — *for programmers*
🔗 typing.io

**What it is:** A typing tutor for developers. Instead of drills, you **type through real open-source code** in JavaScript, Ruby, C, C++, Java, PHP, Perl, Haskell, Scala, and more.

**Why it's different**
- Includes the awkward keys other tutors skip: **symbols and backspace**, which gives more realistic (lower) WPM.
- Practices real syntax/indentation patterns like `{}`, `()`, `=>`, `[]`, `;`.
- Free tier + a paid Pro plan ⚠️ (pricing varies by source; verify).

**Pros**
- Directly relevant if you write code for a living/degree.
- Reveals the real bottleneck: symbols, not letters.

**Cons**
- Narrow — won't teach you touch typing fundamentals.
- Code samples are fixed (not adaptive).
- Can feel old/rough compared to newer tools.

**Best for:** Developers who already type decently (40+ WPM) and want to cut typos in code.

---

### 4.8 Ratatype — *lessons + a verifiable certificate*
🔗 ratatype.com

**What it is:** A free online typing tutor with structured courses (multiple languages/layouts) and a **shareable typing certificate** (bronze/silver/gold) that others can verify via a unique link.

**Key features**
- Structured lessons (roughly 15–20 per course, each with multiple exercises), rewards for completing, zero-error, and speed.
- Posture and finger-placement guidance up front.
- Game mode (earn coins, unlock heroes).
- Free version has some daily limits on certain exercises; paid options remove limits/ads ⚠️.

**Pros**
- The only mainstream site whose **certificate** is designed to be shared/verified.
- Friendly for beginners and classrooms.

**Cons**
- Basic statistics; fixed curriculum.
- Forces you to repeat exercises if you make too many errors (good for accuracy, annoying for some).

**Best for:** Beginners who want structure or need a certificate for a resume/application.

## 6. A Practical Plan (12 Weeks)

### Week 0 — Baseline
Take **5 tests** on Monkeytype (English 200, 60 s). Record: **WPM, raw WPM, accuracy, consistency**. Average them. That's your starting number.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–3)
- **If you don't touch type yet:** TypingClub (or Ratatype) 20 min/day. **Don't look at the keyboard.**
- **If you do:** Keybr 15–20 min/day.
- **Goal:** Accuracy **≥ 95–97%** before chasing speed.

### Phase 2 — Build Speed (Weeks 4–8)
Daily (30–40 min total):
1. **5 min warm-up** — slow, clean typing (Monkeytype 15–30 words).
2. **15 min Keybr** — weakness lessons.
3. **10 min Monkeytype** — burst tests (10 words / 15 s) + a few 60 s tests.
4. **5–10 min TypeRacer / Nitro Type** — fun + pressure.

**Goal:** +3–5 WPM every 2 weeks while keeping accuracy ≥ 95%.

### Phase 3 — Real-World + Polish (Weeks 9–12)
- Add **punctuation, numbers, and quotes** on Monkeytype.
- If you code: 5–10 min daily on **Typing.io** or Monkeytype code mode (JavaScript, Java, etc.).
- Do **one long test** (120 s) weekly for endurance.
- Re-run your baseline (5 × 60 s tests) and compare averages.

### Weekly schedule template

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | Keybr 20 min + Monkeytype bursts |
| Tue | Keybr 15 min + Monkeytype 60 s tests |
| Wed | Monkeytype quotes/punctuation + TypeRacer |
| Thu | Keybr 15 min + code typing |
| Fri | Burst training + Nitro Type/TypeRacer for fun |
| Sat | Long test (120 s) + review stats |
| Sun | Rest or light 10-min session |

## 7. Techniques That Actually Move the Needle

1. **Accuracy first.** Fast + accurate beats fast + sloppy. Backspacing costs far more time than typing slowly and cleanly.
2. **Don't look at the keyboard.** Even a little peeking caps your speed. Cover it or practice with the keys "invisible."
3. **Proper finger assignment** (each finger owns specific keys). Bad habits are the #1 reason people plateau at 50–60 WPM.
4. **Read ahead.** Keep your eyes 1–2 words ahead of your fingers.
5. **Keep rhythm.** Even, steady keystrokes beat fast/slow spikes. Watch Monkeytype's **consistency** number.
6. **Burst training.** Short, all-out tests (10 words / 15 s) train your fastest speed; longer tests train sustained speed.
7. **Deliberate weakness practice.** Slow down on error-prone bigrams/keys (Keybr does this automatically).
8. **Short daily > long occasional.** 20–40 focused minutes daily beats a 3-hour weekend cram.
9. **Stop when tired.** Practicing while fatigued reinforces mistakes.
10. **Ergonomics.** Neutral wrists, relaxed shoulders, decent keyboard. Pain = stop and fix your setup.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Comparing scores across different sites.
- ❌ Chasing personal bests on easy word lists (leads to plateaus).
- ❌ Only using racing/game sites (volume without technique).
- ❌ Ignoring accuracy ("I'll fix it later" — you won't).
- ❌ Using autotype/scripts on leaderboards (they exist for Monkeytype, but they defeat the whole purpose and are cheating).
- ❌ Practicing only letters and never punctuation/numbers.
- ❌ Judging yourself on one test — use the **average of your last 5–10 tests**.

## 9. How to Track Progress Properly

Keep a simple log (Notes app or spreadsheet), weekly:

| Date | Test | Avg WPM (5 tests) | Raw | Accuracy | Consistency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| … | Monkeytype English 200, 60 s | | | | | |

Watch these signals:
- **Raw − WPM gap large?** → Accuracy is your bottleneck. Slow down.
- **Consistency low?** → Rhythm problem; smooth out your typing.
- **WPM stuck for 2+ weeks?** → Change something: harder text (quotes/punctuation), burst tests, or a targeted weakness session.
